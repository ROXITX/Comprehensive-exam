# method
S: 🧭 Data science methodology (IBM)
> A 10-step recipe that loops:
> 1. Business understanding — what's the problem?
> 2. Analytic approach — how can data answer it?
> 3. Data requirements · 4. Data collection
> 5. Data understanding · 6. Data preparation (takes the most time)
> 7. Modeling · 8. Evaluation · 9. Deployment · 10. Feedback
S: Four kinds of analytics
> - **Descriptive**: what happened?
> - **Diagnostic**: why?
> - **Predictive**: what will happen?
> - **Prescriptive**: what should we do?
> CRISP-DM is a similar 6-phase process.

# competing
S: 🏆 Competing on Analytics (Davenport)
> **Analytical competitors** use data as their main weapon, across the whole company, backed by top management.
> Examples: Capital One (test & learn), Harrah's (loyalty), Netflix (recommendations), Walmart & UPS (supply chain), Oakland A's (Moneyball).
S: Internal vs external processes
> - **Internal**: finance, M&A, operations, R&D, **HR/talent**
> - **External**: **customers** (pricing, loyalty, marketing) and **suppliers** (supply chain)
S: Stages & DELTA
> Five stages: Impaired → Localised → Aspirations → Analytical companies → **Analytical competitors**.
> **DELTA**: Data, Enterprise, Leadership, Targets, Analysts.

# viz
S: 📊 Picking the right chart
> - Trend over time → **line**
> - Compare categories → **bar**
> - Shape of one variable → **histogram**

# linregds
S: 📉 Regression for business
> Predict a number, e.g. Sales = 200 + 15 × AdSpend → AdSpend 10 → **350**.
> The coefficient = change in Y per unit of X (holding the others fixed).
S: In Python
> - `np.polyfit(x, y, 1)` → [slope, intercept]
> - scikit-learn: `.fit()`, `.coef_`, `.intercept_`, `.predict()`, `.score()` (R²)
> - Formula: b = (XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy
> Check residual plots (patterns = a bad fit). Turn categories into **dummy variables**.

# linopt
S: 📐 Linear programming
> Choose amounts (**decision variables**) to maximise or minimise a linear **objective** under linear **constraints**.
> 💡 How many chairs and tables to make with limited wood and labour?
S: Solving with corners
> The best answer is always at a **corner** of the feasible region.
> Max 3x + 2y with x + y ≤ 4, x + 3y ≤ 6: corners give 0, 12, 11, 4 → **12 at (4, 0)**.
S: Words to know
> - **Infeasible**: no point fits all constraints
> - **Unbounded**: can improve forever
> - **Binding constraint**: fully used (slack 0)
> - **Shadow price**: gain from one more unit of a resource
> `scipy.optimize.linprog` **minimises** → negate to maximise. The simplex method hops between corners.

# numpy
S: 🔢 NumPy arrays
> `a = np.array([[1, 2], [3, 4]])` → `a.shape` = (2, 2).
> - `a * a` → **element-wise** [[1, 4], [9, 16]]
> - `a @ a` → **matrix product** [[7, 10], [15, 22]]
S: Linear algebra
> - `a.T` transpose · `np.linalg.det(a)` = −2
> - `np.linalg.inv(a)` · `np.linalg.solve(A, b)` solves Ax = b
> - `np.linalg.eig` · `np.linalg.norm([3, 4])` = 5 · `np.eye(3)` identity
S: Shapes & axes
> - (2 × 3) @ (3 × 4) → (2 × 4)
> - **Broadcasting**: (3, 1) + (1, 4) → (3, 4)
> - `sum(axis=0)` → per column; `sum(axis=1)` → per row

# series
S: 📏 Pandas Series
> A **labelled** 1-D list: values + **index**.
> `s = pd.Series([10, 20, 30], index=['a', 'b', 'c'])`
> - `s['b']` → 20 (label) · `s.iloc[0]` → 10 (position)
> - From a dict: the keys become the index
S: Handy behaviour
> - Maths is element-wise: `s * 2`
> - Adding two Series **lines up by index** → unmatched labels give **NaN**
> - `value_counts()`, `isnull()`, `fillna()`, `nunique()`
> - `mean()` skips NaN

# dataframe
S: 🧮 Pandas DataFrame
> A table: rows × columns; each column is a Series.
> - `df.shape` → (rows, cols) · `df.head()` · `df.info()`
> - `df.describe()` → count, mean, std, min, 25%, 50%, 75%, max
S: Selecting data
> - `df['col']` → Series; `df[['col']]` → DataFrame
> - **loc** = labels, slice **includes** the end (`loc[0:2]` → 3 rows)
> - **iloc** = positions, slice **excludes** the end (`iloc[0:2]` → 2 rows)
> - Filter: `df[(df.age > 30) & (df.city == 'Pune')]`
S: Reshaping & cleaning
> - `groupby('dept')['salary'].mean()`
> - `pd.merge(a, b, on='id', how='left')` (SQL joins) · `pd.concat` stacks
> - `dropna()`, `fillna(0)`, `drop(columns=…)`, `sort_values(…)`

# prob
S: 🎲 Discrete distributions (counting)
> - **Binomial**(n, p): successes in n tries — mean np, variance np(1 − p)
> - **Poisson**(λ): events per interval — mean = variance = λ
> - **Geometric**: tries until the first success
> - **Bernoulli**: a single yes/no trial
S: Continuous distributions (measuring)
> - **Normal**: bell curve — 68% / 95% / 99.7% within 1 / 2 / 3 SD
> - **Uniform**[a, b]: mean (a + b)/2
> - **Exponential**: waiting time, mean 1/λ, **memoryless**
> For continuous variables, P(X = exact value) = 0.
S: z-scores & CLT
> **z = (x − μ)/σ** (70 average, SD 10, score 85 → z = 1.5).
> **Central Limit Theorem**: averages of large samples look normal.
> PMF = discrete, PDF = continuous.

# eda
S: 🔍 Exploratory Data Analysis
> Look at your data **before** modelling (John Tukey's idea): shape, missing values, outliers, relationships.
> Pandas: `info()`, `describe()`, `isnull().sum()`, `corr()`.
S: Centre & spread
> - Mean (pulled by outliers) vs **median** (robust) vs mode
> - Standard deviation, **IQR = Q3 − Q1**
> - Outliers: below Q1 − 1.5·IQR or above Q3 + 1.5·IQR
S: Shape & relationships
> - Mean > median → **right-skewed**
> - Histograms and box plots for one variable; scatter plots and heatmaps for pairs
> - **Correlation ≠ causation** (ice cream and drowning share a cause: summer)
